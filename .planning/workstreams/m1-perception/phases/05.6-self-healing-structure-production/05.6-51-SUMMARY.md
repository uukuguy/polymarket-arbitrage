# 05.6-51 Summary — bounded Quote persistence and full incident history

## Outcome

Production evidence showed Quote collection could complete its fetch and
transform stages, acquire SQLite's writer, and still spend the remaining
180-second child lifetime in one large atomic terminal-write transaction.
The existing SQLite `timeout` bounded lock acquisition only.

`NegRiskQuoteStore` now installs an absolute SQLite progress-handler deadline
for its writer connection. A bulk write that exceeds the configured 15-second
writer budget is interrupted and rolled back atomically. The collector
classifies that `interrupted` result as the existing typed persistence timeout,
so the child terminalizes cleanly and the supervised producer retries.

The Fly-native incident console now includes `structure` in its retained
severe-incident history. Operators can inspect a Structure P1's current
checkpoint and its complete recovered lifecycle beside Quote and capacity
evidence.

## Verification

- RED/GREEN store test proves an over-deadline terminal write is interrupted
  and leaves no partial Quote rows.
- RED/GREEN collector test proves both SQLite lock and absolute-deadline
  interruption produce `QuotePersistenceTimeoutError`.
- Focused Quote store, collector, worker and perception-console regressions
  passed (165 tests).
- Changed-file Ruff, `make docs-m1-check`, `make dashboard-build`, and
  `make planning-status` passed.

## Production follow-up

Deploy once without an overlapping Fly release. The next overly long Quote
persist must become `child-persist-timeout`, not `child-hard-timeout`; then
observe a later certified Quote generation, opportunity-feed recovery, and
closure of the corresponding incident lifecycle. Keep Structure P1 open until
its generation publishes and a fresh certified Quote consumes that receipt.
