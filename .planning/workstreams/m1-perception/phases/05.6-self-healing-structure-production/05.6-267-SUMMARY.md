# Plan 05.6-267 Summary: Recover Recurring Quote Production

The exact release `3a70cd9f5a52294fba5709f0d390421600baa5de` was built and
pushed from OrbStack as image digest
`sha256:870f312ffe7786bc9a842812ec86275ec30334e6219cbb3f1b84fd6d61fc2748`.
All eight existing Fly Machines retained their IDs and non-image configuration,
updated under fresh optimistic versions, reached `started`, and passed the
repository's exact machine-update verifier. Qualification alone received the
new release identity.

Before rollout, Q1 had 140 inputs, 140 receipts and 140 succeeded producers but
its certifier was still `waiting`. Updating the coordinator caused the repaired
durable sweep to certify and publish that exact generation without operator SQL.
Because the blocked prices had genuinely aged beyond 900 seconds, its
Opportunity successor correctly quarantined with `StaleQuoteGenerationError`.
This was a contained data-product outcome, not a reason to restart the system or
relax freshness.

The next cadence run Q2 reused the same certified Structure, completed 140/140
Quote batches, published Quote at `23:04:00.730309+00`, and published Opportunity
29.768514 seconds later. Its oldest quoted batch was only about four and a half
minutes old at Opportunity certification. Production then admitted Q3 and kept
normalizing the next Structure generation; open circuits and expired leases
remained zero, the control API stayed available, and the runtime controller was
healthy.

Climb H-062 passed all nine local subscores at 100% with
`disaster_pattern=false`. The new learning chapter and architecture-thread entry
record why Structure and Quote require separate clocks, why durable fan-in is a
normal correctness path, and why stale Q1 isolation plus Q2 continuation is the
intended production playbook.
