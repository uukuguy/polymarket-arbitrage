# Plan 05.6-211 Summary: One Absolute Circuit Clock

After revision 033 and the new coordinator image were live, the exact recovery
preflight showed one remaining action but a durable `last_next_allowed_at`
about sixteen hours in the future. The central retry policy caps a Structure
retry at 300 seconds, so this was not a legitimate backoff.

The live projection calculated `cooldown_seconds` as `next_probe_at -
opened_at`. After every failed probe it then scheduled the budget deadline as
`now + cooldown_seconds`. Because `opened_at` remains the original trip time,
elapsed open age was repeatedly added to the present. Two individually valid
timestamps therefore formed a compounding second clock.

Runtime reconciliation now carries the circuit's absolute `next_probe_at` and
compares it directly with `now`. Circuit recovery candidates use zero budget
cooldown, and `schedule_action()` rejects a nonzero circuit cooldown. Revision
034 clears only derived circuit-budget deadlines; job-target cooldowns and
remaining action counts are unchanged. Historical observe payloads retain a
one-way compatibility translation, while live facts never reconstruct the old
relative clock.

Verification:

- real PostgreSQL 033→034→033 migration preserved job cooldown and cleared only
  circuit budget deadlines;
- integration proof used an opened-at timestamp one day old and retained the
  exact 300-second next probe without compounding;
- the runtime fault matrix passed 15/15, including closed rows carrying stale
  timestamp fields;
- changed files passed Ruff and Pyright with zero diagnostics;
- fresh `make test-m1` passed **4,030 tests, one skip and one expected xfail in
  1,574.08 seconds**, without an outer timeout.

Production is revision 033. Coordinator `e82d1220b2d138` runs exact Plan 210
release `65cee5b9`; controller is stopped, while Structure, Quote and
